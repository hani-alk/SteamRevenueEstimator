How it's calculated
  1. First-week copies wishlists × (1 − late share ÷ 2) × conversion × beyond wishlists Since Steam's Personal Calendar hit the homepage, a game with a public date picks up a wave of drive-by wishlists in its final eight weeks. Those are counted at half weight: the conversion rate you set is for wishlists held longer than that.
  2. First-year copies first week × year-one sales A 2.5× game sells one and a half times its opening week again over the following eleven months.
  3. First-month copies first week × 4 ^ (ln(year-one sales) ÷ ln 52) Sales pile up on a power curve rather than a straight line, anchored so that week 52 lands exactly on the first-year figure. The month — and the curve in the panel — read off the same formula.
  4. Copies you're paid for copies × (1 − refund rate)
  5. Sale price at each milestone price × (1 − erosion × (0.5 + 0.5 × share of the year sold)) The first week feels half the erosion you set — regional pricing and VAT apply from day one, but the deep discounts come later. By week 52 it feels all of it, and the month lands on the ramp between.
  6. Gross revenue copies paid for × average sale price
  7. Steam's cut 30% to $10M · 25% to $50M · 20% above Tiers count from your first dollar, so each milestone is charged on its own gross.
  8. Withholding (gross − Steam's cut) × treaty rate
  9. Take-home gross − Steam's cut − withholding

Assumptions

Late wishlists convert at half
There's no public data yet on calendar-era wishlists; half is a guess. Set the share to 0% to switch it off.
Steam's cut is tiered
30% up to $10M lifetime gross, 25% to $50M, 20% above. Almost every indie sits in the first tier for its whole life.
Gross is already net of VAT and sales tax
Steam's price in most regions includes VAT, which Valve strips before splitting the money. That, plus regional pricing, is why the erosion slider defaults well above zero.
Refunds are removed as copies
A refund costs you the whole sale, not a slice of it. Steam's revenue share is charged on what's left.
Everything stops at twelve months
No guess is made about what the game earns after that.
This is revenue, not profit
Steam Direct, ports, marketing and your own costs aren't modelled.
Withholding follows your W-8BEN
Treaty rates vary by country and the list above is a shortcut, not advice — confirm yours with an accountant. US developers file a W-9 and see none.
Money arrives about a month later
Valve pays each month's earnings around the 30th of the following month, once you're over $100.
Exchange rates are approximate
Type today's rate over the default if it matters.
