How it's calculated
  1. First-week units wishlists × conversion × word of mouth
  2. First-year units first week × year-one sales A 2.5× game sells one and a half times its opening week again over the following eleven months.
  3. First-month units first week × 4 ^ (ln(year-one sales) ÷ ln 52) Sales pile up on a power curve rather than a straight line, anchored so that week 52 lands exactly on the first-year figure. The month reads off the same curve, so nothing here depends on guessing how long the game sells for.
  4. Units you're paid for units × (1 − refund rate)
  5. Sale price at each milestone price × (1 − erosion × (0.5 + 0.5 × share of the year sold)) The first week feels half the erosion you set — regional pricing applies from day one, but the deep discounts come later. By week 52 it feels all of it, and the month lands on the ramp between.
  6. Gross revenue units paid for × average sale price
  7. Steam's cut 30% to $10M · 25% to $50M · 20% above Tiers count from your first dollar, so each milestone is charged on its own gross.
  8. Withholding (gross − Steam's cut) × treaty rate
  9. Take-home gross − Steam's cut − withholding

Assumptions

Steam's cut is tiered
30% up to $10M gross, 25% to $50M, 20% above.
Gross is already net of VAT
That's how Valve reports it, so there's no separate line.
Refunds are removed as units
A refund costs you the whole sale, not a slice of it.
Everything stops at twelve months
No guess is made about what the game earns after that.
This is revenue, not profit
Steam Direct, ports, marketing and your own costs aren't modelled.
Withholding follows your W-8BEN
Treaty rates vary by country. Confirm yours with an accountant.
Exchange rates are approximate
Type today's rate over the default if it matters.
